Perspective Compass
Case 3: feed and recommender design against filter-bubble reinforcement

How can a recommender inform users in a way that is varied but still topically relevant, instead of reinforcing bubbles?

  • Team 13, BHH: Felix, Erik, Dogan
  • International Design Thinking & Entrepreneurship Workshop Hamburg, 31.08.–12.09.2026
  • Topic partner: digi&demo e.V. (project "pleenum")
Working title. "Perspective Compass" is a working name only. The final product name and the logo are still a team decision and are not part of this deck.
Team 13 · Perspective Compass1 / 12
The problem · the Why

Engagement-optimised feeds reinforce bubbles

  • "For You" feeds on the big platforms are optimised for engagement, not for a balanced picture. That reinforces filter bubbles and self-selected echo chambers.
  • Two different failure modes in the same target group:
    • Some users do not notice they are in a bubble and consider their feed balanced.
    • Others do notice, try to break out, and the algorithm keeps pulling them back to similar content.
  • Why it matters: reduced exposure to diverse but relevant perspectives weakens healthy democratic discourse. This is the core concern of digi&demo e.V.

Who it is for

Two personas, one target group

Mia, 20, "The Everyperson"

Checks Instagram and TikTok many times a day, mostly follows accounts that match her views, sees herself as informed. Does not connect the occasional surprise at a friend's opinion to her feed.

Need: the bubble made visible without having to look for it.

Tom, 22, "The Seeker"

More media-literate, follows a few accounts on purpose to diversify, feels like he is fighting the algorithm.

Need: a tool that reliably supplies topically relevant counter-perspectives without flooding him with irrelevant content.

  • Primary: young adults (18–25) who consume political and societal content mainly through algorithmic feeds.
  • Secondary: educators, associations and initiatives (digi&demo e.V., "Netz Macht Politik") who could use the tool as demonstration and media-literacy material.
Personas are a target-group description, not validated by real interviews yet.

The solution · the How

One feed, two switchable modes

Flask frontend, classic content-based filtering (TF-IDF and cosine similarity), no self-trained ML model.

  • Standard mode: bubble-reinforcing. For a logged-in account it reads the like history, so the majority perspective of everything liked so far steers the feed and it self-reinforces with every further like, on purpose without a built-in way out. This is the key demo scene.
  • Diversity-aware mode: same similarity basis, but every few positions it deliberately mixes in the closest post with the same topic and the opposing perspective, marked "Suggested", and uses the same account tendency to break it.
  • Two independent axes made visible: pro/contra perspective on the topic, plus a self-declared political label (left/centre/right), chosen by the author, not guessed by the app.
  • Visible diversity score per feed view, our answer to the "we need a metric" critical issue.
  • Real accounts, likes and comments (Supabase), plus a read-only section of public Mastodon posts on the current topic.
Team 13 · Perspective Compass4 / 12
Scope

What we deliberately left out

  • No self-trained ML recommender. Classic content-based filtering is enough for the core question and realistic in two weeks without ML experience in the team.
  • No automatic left/right classifier. Reliable political classification from free text is an unsolved, contested problem, and an app that silently claims "this is objectively left" would reproduce exactly the invisible algorithmic judgement the case is about. Users self-label instead.
  • Not a replacement for anyone's TikTok or Instagram feed. It is a standalone demonstrator that shows the mechanism.
  • No full ActivityPub implementation. The Fediverse section is read-only via Mastodon's public API. A full client/server stays a concept sketch.

Onboarding concept

Feel the bubble before the fix is named

Four steps, built so "Mia" types experience the problem before the solution is shown:

  • 1. Topic selection: the user picks 2–3 topics from the curated example set to create a realistic starting feed.
  • 2. Standard feed first: the "Standard" mode without any explanation, so the bubble effect is experienced rather than described.
  • 3. Perspective compass: after a little interaction, a simple visualisation of how one-sided the feed has been so far (the diversity metric).
  • 4. Diversity mode offered: only then the switch to "Diversity-aware", with visible marking of which posts were mixed in as counter-perspectives.
Team 13 · Perspective Compass6 / 12
Positioning and marketing strategy

A transparency tool, not another social app

Core message: "Your feed helps decide which opinions you see. We show you how, and what a fairer feed could look like instead."
  • Positioning: it shows the difference between bubble-reinforcing and diversity-aware ranking side by side instead of just claiming to be fair.
  • Persona-split messaging:
    • For "Mia" types: low-threshold and curiosity-driven ("test how one-sided your feed really is"), not lecturing.
    • For "Tom" types: concrete and tool-oriented ("finally a feed that does not pull you back").
  • Channels: digi&demo e.V. and the "pleenum" community as the first fitting multiplier, the "Netz Macht Politik" network, the Innovation Fair (10./11.09.), the workshop blog post, the market stand.
  • Honest reach framing: currently a standalone demo. Marketing promises "shows the mechanism", not "replaces your feed".

Social Business Model Canvas · condensed

Business model

Value proposition: makes visible how strongly a ranking algorithm reinforces your bubble, and shows a topically relevant alternative. Visibility for Mia, break-out for Tom.

Beneficiaries: young adults (18–25) with mostly algorithmic news consumption; secondary educational institutions and associations such as digi&demo e.V.

Channels: direct via digi&demo e.V., the Bürgerhaus action-day network, the workshop's own channels.

Customer relationship: open and explanatory, transparency as the core promise.

Key partners: digi&demo e.V. (content, expertise, reach), possibly Bürgerhaus Wilhelmsburg, ITECH-BS14 as the first test context.

Key activities: curating and labelling the example dataset, maintaining the two ranking modes, developing the comparison frontend, outreach.

Key resources: the Flask stack and team know-how from the Pi-Dashboard project, the curated dataset, the digi&demo contact.

Cost structure: development time (unpaid within the workshop), hosting for a later demo version, dataset upkeep.

Funding: no direct revenue planned (non-profit, awareness). For a real continuation: funding through digi&demo e.V. or similar initiatives, or licensing the comparison logic to open platforms (Bluesky custom feeds).

Social impact: makes bubble mechanisms visible to a group that does not notice them, gives the other group a tool to break out. Media literacy and digital participation.

Team 13 · Perspective Compass8 / 12
Competition and differentiation

Complement, not competitor

  • digi&demo e.V. "pleenum": focuses on discourse format and moderation. We focus on the feed ranking itself. Communicate as a complement.
  • Community-Notes-style and "bridging" recommender experiments: adjacent research direction. Our niche is making the mechanism visible plus a switchable alternative in the same interface.
  • Big platforms: optimise for engagement by design. We deliberately show the opposite and make it legible.

Roadmap

Next steps

  • Near term: apply the political-label migration against the live database, seed demo accounts with opposing like histories, verify the Fediverse section against the real Mastodon API, add a diversity-over-time view.
  • Medium term: pilot with digi&demo e.V. as teaching and demonstration material, gather real user feedback, decide on a final name and visual identity.
