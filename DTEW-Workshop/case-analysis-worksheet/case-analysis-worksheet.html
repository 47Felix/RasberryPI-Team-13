The Internet and Politics – Your Network, Your Rules?

Case Analysis (filled in, Team 13)

Task: Choose 2–3 cases, identify the key problems, and think about possible solutions.
Our direction is digi&demo e.V. (digiunddemo.de), connected via the action day "Dein Netz, Deine Regeln!" at Bürgerhaus Wilhelmsburg. digi&demo names three challenges to open digital discourse on their own site – those are our three cases, all seen through digi&demo's lens (their booth activities and their project "pleenum").
CASE 1digi&demo – Hate speech
CASE 2digi&demo – Disinformation
CASE 3digi&demo – Polarization

Ideas on Case 1: digi&demo, Hate speech

Core problems
  1. Aggressive/toxic comments under political posts discourage people from participating.
  2. Reporting tools on major platforms feel slow and impersonal.
  3. Those affected often face coordinated pile-ons ("shitstorms") rather than single comments – hard to fight alone.
Who is affected?
Young people/young adults who speak up politically – especially those from groups more frequently targeted by hate.
Possible solutions
  1. Moderated discussion spaces with clear rules (similar to digi&demo's "pleenum").
  2. Community-driven counter-speech tools.
  3. Visible status after a report is filed ("reported → reviewed → outcome").

Ideas on Case 2: digi&demo, Disinformation

Reference: digi&demo's own booth game tests visitors' ability to spot manipulated content.

Core problems
  1. Young people/young adults struggle to tell manipulated/fake content from real content.
  2. Disinformation undermines trust in political debate.
  3. Existing awareness campaigns often don't reach the target group (too dry/theoretical).
Who is affected?
Young people/first-time voters who get political information via social media.
Possible solutions
  1. A gamified "spot-the-fake" trainer (similar to digi&demo's game), e.g. as a browser extension.
  2. Short, everyday-usable explainer snippets instead of long educational texts.
  3. A community-based fact-check feature (similar to Community Notes).

Ideas on Case 3: digi&demo, Polarization

Reference: digi&demo's own project "pleenum" directly targets open, constructive digital discourse instead of camp-building.

Core problems
  1. Algorithms/filter bubbles reinforce one-sided views (echo chambers).
  2. Political discussions escalate quickly into "us vs. them" instead of real exchange.
  3. Constructive exchange across differing opinions is rare online.
Who is affected?
Social media users who take political positions – especially young people who are still forming their political opinions.
Possible solutions
  1. A structured discourse format modeled on pleenum (show the opposing view before you can reply).
  2. Nudges that encourage reading an opposing view before commenting.
  3. Moderated cross-bubble formats that deliberately bring different political camps together.

Tip (from the original worksheet): Focus on the problem first. Good solution ideas often become clearer once you know who is affected and why.
